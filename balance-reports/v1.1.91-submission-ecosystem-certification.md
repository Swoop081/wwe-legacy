# v1.1.91 — Submission Ecosystem Certification + Diagnostic Integrity

## Purpose
Validate whether WWE Legacy's pin-heavy finish distribution is a systemic balance defect or a natural consequence of roster composition, while respecting the locked rule that persistent body-part damage is banked across repeated submission applications.

## Audit sample
- Active field: 82 Superstars
- Pairings: all 3,321 active-82 pairings
- Games sampled: 8 per pairing
- Matches: 26,568
- Stalls: 0
- Average turns: 25.51
- Pin finishes: 23,919
- Submission finishes: 2,649
- Submission share: 9.97%

## Main finding
The approximately 10% overall submission finish rate is **not evidence that submission specialists are systemically broken**. Pins are a universal finish route available to every Superstar, while submission victories are concentrated among a smaller specialist population.

Submission specialists convert a large portion of their wins through their intended route:
- Kurt Angle: 61.0% of wins by submission
- Bret Hart: 56.7%
- Owen Hart: 55.0%
- Rowdy Roddy Piper: 51.3%
- Charlotte Flair: 49.1%
- Chris Jericho: 43.2%
- Ted DiBiase: 45.2%
- Doink the Clown: 38.9%
- Gunther: 38.6%

This is healthy identity separation: specialists can genuinely win through submissions without forcing the whole game toward an artificial pin/submission split.

## Diagnostic correction
The older `ai-submission-strategy-audit.mjs` labelled a start as `doomed` whenever the remaining pressure required exceeded the attacker's current hand size. That label was conceptually wrong for WWE Legacy because body-part damage persists after a hold is released. A hold can deliberately bank pressure, retain Control, and make a later application lethal.

v1.1.91 changes the diagnostic vocabulary to:
- `repeatApplicationRequired`: the current application cannot finish inside its authored hold cadence and is functioning as setup.
- `handLimitedStarts`: the remaining pressure exceeds the current hand size, but this is **not** treated as failure because future applications can use the persistent damage.

No gameplay rule has been changed to improve a metric.

## Pin timing
The separate AI pin-timing audit produced 20,604 attempts with an average escape chance of 46.8%. There were zero attempts in the 0–19% escape-chance bands. Pin AI therefore does not show an equivalent pathological 'attempt hopelessly early' problem.

## Decision
- Do **not** globally buff submissions.
- Do **not** reduce pin strength simply to increase submission share.
- Keep submissionThreshold tied to current HP.
- Keep persistent body-part damage.
- Keep defender draw and response windows.
- Continue balancing individual submission specialists through authentic card/ability identity when one specifically underperforms.
- Treat overall finish mix as a roster-composition metric, not a mandatory target ratio.

## Baseline impact
Gameplay remains the v1.1.90 architecture baseline. v1.1.91 is a diagnostics/certification integrity release: it improves our ability to interpret submission simulations without introducing a false balance correction.
